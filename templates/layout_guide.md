# Layout Components

Use these components only when they make the article easier to understand. The publishing step styles them. Do not add inline styles, scripts, a manual table of contents, or decorative boxes.

- **Quick answer**: use at most once, after the opening paragraph, when a short direct answer helps.
  `<div class="quick-answer"><p>Two or three sentences that answer the main question.</p></div>`
- **Tip**: use only for a useful action the reader may otherwise miss. At most 2.
  `<div class="tip"><p>One practical tip.</p></div>`
- **Heads up**: use only for a real limit, cost, risk or important check. At most 2.
  `<div class="warning"><p>What to watch out for.</p></div>`
- **Steps**: use for a task that must be completed in order.
  `<ol class="steps"><li><strong>Do this.</strong> Explain the step in plain English.</li></ol>`
- **Comparison table**: use only when comparing three or more options across at least three useful points. Keep cells brief and every fact must come from verified research.

Most of the article should be normal paragraphs with clear `<h2>` and `<h3>` headings. Do not make the page look like a dashboard, catalogue or directory. Avoid stacking callout boxes and lists.
